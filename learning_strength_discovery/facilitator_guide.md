# Facilitator guide

Use this guide with the original Learning Strength Discovery pilot for grades 5–8. The purpose is a useful conversation and a provisional learning-habit profile. The kit does not establish stable personality traits, intelligence, attention capacity, or clinical findings.

## Prepare

Read the student prompts and activity cards before the visit. Have paper, a pencil, and the materials named on the cards. Agree who will see responses and how long records will be kept. Explain those arrangements to the student and establish appropriate participation arrangements for your setting before collecting information. This package contains no online data-collection service; your handling arrangements determine who sees completed records.

Offer spoken responses, larger text, help with reading, or another suitable format. Record the help provided. Access support is available throughout and is separate from a hint about solving a task. Do not ask for a diagnosis to make a task accessible.

Explain that the student can pause, skip, disagree, or stop. Help and breaks do not require earning a reward. Never use a timer as pressure or deliberately create frustration. The total visit estimate of 25–35 minutes is an unmeasured planning assumption; split the visit when useful.

## Ask and observe

Use the recent-time anchor and response labels printed in the questionnaire. Ask for the student's experience rather than a desirable answer. Preserve depends, not sure, and skipped responses as distinct outcomes. Offer neutral clarification without coaching a response.

If a student combines a support choice with an unknown, skip, or no-support answer, offer one neutral chance to clarify. Preserve an unresolved mixed response as unclear. Do not silently select the answer you think they meant.

The personality prompts are short reflections on different tendencies. A single response cannot establish a stable trait. Sociability and cooperation prompts describe reported preferences or behavior; individual puzzles do not demonstrate peer cooperation.

Read the task instructions as written and offer the stated optional supports. Keep a record of which task, phase, materials, access supports, and content hints were available. Note relevant interruptions reported or observed. The activities are curriculum-light, but language, experience, and familiarity can still affect them.

Record actions such as starting, explaining an idea, requesting a hint, revising an answer, checking a requirement, or choosing to pause. Avoid labels such as distracted, lazy, anxious, gifted, or low ability. Silence and response time do not establish the cause of a difficulty.

## Interpret without a total score

Keep selected response words. Do not add responses, average facets, assign cutoffs, reverse-score items, or produce a percentage. The questionnaire was not designed as a validated scale. Skips and unknowns are never zeros or negative evidence.

For an activity record these events separately:

- Whether the student attempted the phase or chose to stop.
- Whether they reached a solution before a task-content hint.
- Which hint or example preceded any later solution.
- What they explained in their own words.
- Whether they applied the rule or checked requirements on a new example.

Use the facilitator key to describe the answer on that task. A mistaken answer is an opportunity to ask about the approach. It does not establish a deficit. Keep access supports visible in the record without treating their use as reduced ability.

Do not call a second attempt evidence that a support caused improvement. Practice, order, familiarity, fatigue, and different task demands can explain differences. Write "completed after a hint" rather than "needs hints to learn."

## Build the report

Complete every dimension in the report template. Unknown is a valid result. Each entry includes a student report or observation, its context, what remains uncertain, and a suggestion to discuss. Do not fill missing facets with a model's guess.

Use these evidence descriptions:

- Reported preference or habit: the student said it. Keep its recent-time context.
- Observed in this activity: a recorded action under stated conditions.
- Tentative interpretation: a hypothesis the student can question.
- Not yet known: no suitable evidence, or unresolved conflict.

Multiple observations may justify another check, but repetition within one visit does not establish reliability or generality. Avoid a numerical confidence score. If the same event supports two dimensions, retain the same source rather than treating it as independent evidence twice.

For example, the student can report difficulty starting at home and begin promptly when a task has a clear first step. Keep both findings. A possible trial is a visible first step during later learning, checked in that context.

## Review with the student before planning

Ask the student to describe the proposed support in their own words and say what they would change. Agreement is not proof that an interpretation is valid. Correct misrecorded answers, retain conflicting contexts, and leave unresolved interpretations tentative.

If the student disputes a finding, explain which suggestions depended on it. Revise or withdraw those suggestions as appropriate. Offer a choice to try a support without requiring agreement with a trait interpretation. Record the correction and the report version used for later decisions.

## Propose arrangements to try

Ask about realistic available days and time separately from the assessment findings. Agree on daily duration, focus blocks and pauses, encouragement or optional rewards, supports, and preferred stretch. Exact minutes are collaboratively chosen trial values, not a measured attention limit.

Motivation responses suggest topics to discuss, not an automatic reward prescription. Recognition can be private if preferred. A student may value both progress and rewards, or decline rewards. Revisit arrangements using the student's experience and later independent learning evidence. Actual task difficulty and content require subject-readiness assessment.

## Handle incomplete visits

Offer resumption after a pause, a different format for an access barrier, and explicit unknowns when evidence is sparse. If materials or instructions fail, record that failure separately from the student's choices. Provide the report findings that are supported without inventing the rest.

## Evaluate before wider use

Test whether students understand prompts, response options, report explanations, and the correction process. Include grades 5 and 8 and relevant access needs in those checks. Have two facilitators interpret the same anonymized example independently and compare descriptions and suggestions.

Check whether the activities add useful information beyond the conversation, how much time they take, and what students skip. Track changes after corrections. Later, compare trial arrangements using meaningful learning evidence. The kit and its sample report do not themselves establish validity, reliability, fairness, or effectiveness.
