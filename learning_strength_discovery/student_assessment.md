# Learning Strength Discovery

## Pilot student check-in · grades 5–8

**For the adult:** These are original pilot items, not a validated test. Use them as a conversation about learning habits and choices. Do not produce a total score, rank students, or label a child's personality. Recent-time answers, including Q07–Q08, report a particular period. Q09–Q10 and Q24 request arrangements to try. Activities and recording instructions are in [activity cards](activity_cards.md).

**Pilot time estimate:** 8–12 minutes for this check-in, including a short conversation. This estimate has not been tested. Offer two sittings, reading aloud, oral answers, pointing, a scribe, translated directions, or another accessible response method. Ask which method the student wants. Record the method and any wording changes. Do not change an answer on the student's behalf.

Before starting, explain who will see the answers, where they will be kept, and how the student can correct them, using the pilot's actual arrangements. Do not promise confidentiality you cannot provide. This form is not permission to collect identifying or sensitive information. Use the pilot's agreed participation process before administering it.

### Student page: what this is for

We want to learn what helps you learn. We will ask about your recent learning and let you try three small activities. We will talk about what you enjoy, what helps you focus, how you get started, what you do when work is new, and how you like help from other people.

There are no best answers to these questions. Different choices work at different times. The activities have rules, but they are not a race or a grade. You can ask for help, skip a question or activity, take a break, or stop. You do not have to earn help or breaks.

We will show you what we write about you. You can say, “That does not fit me,” change an answer, or ask us to leave something unknown. Tell us only what you want to share. You do not need to explain private things.

**Before we start, the adult tells me who can see my answers:** ____________________

**I would like to:** □ begin □ hear more about this □ take a break □ skip today

### Think about the same recent time

For questions about things you did, think about **the past 14 days**, from __________ to __________. Count learning in class, at home, or while learning a skill. Think about what happened, even if the past two weeks were unusual.

If you had little chance to do something, choose **No chance to try**. You can tell us about an unusual week without saying why.

Optional: This time was □ about usual for me □ unusual for me □ not sure □ prefer to skip.

### Part 1: what happened during those 14 days?

For each line, choose one answer. You can point to or say its label.

| Answer label | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **N — Never or almost never** | It happened on few or none of the chances I had. |
| **S — Sometimes** | It happened on some of the chances I had. |
| **M — Many times** | It happened on many of the chances I had. |
| **A — Almost every time** | It happened on nearly all the chances I had. |
| **D — Depends** | It changed a lot with the situation. |
| **U — Not sure** | I do not know or do not remember. |
| **X — No chance to try** | This situation did not come up for me. |

The letter labels are recording codes, not points. If you choose **Depends**, you may add an example, but you do not have to.

### Part 2: focus conditions and support choices

The first two questions ask what helped during the past 14 days. The next two ask what you want to try today. Different answers can fit different activities. There is no right answer.

When a question allows two choices, combine two actual conditions, actions, or supports if both fit. Choose Depends, Not sure, Prefer to skip, or a no-opportunity answer on its own. Answers saying no help worked, no extra support, or try first also stand alone. You can add an example to explain a choice.

### My say before we finish

Coverage is a design intention, not evidence that the items measure stable traits. **There are 24 top-level question IDs: 19 frequency reports and 5 choice items/groups. Keep the six motive reports separate. They are not opposing ends of one scale, and they must not be summed into “high” or “low motivation.” Keep missing responses distinct: **Depends**, **Not sure**, **No chance to try**, and **Prefer to skip** do not mean “Never.” Scenario choices are requests to try an arrangement, not evidence that the arrangement improves learning. A preference for peers or cooperation is not an observation of either behavior.

Reading, language, disability access, task familiarity, recent opportunities, adult presence, and current circumstances can affect every response. This curriculum-light kit is not culture-free. Follow the separate interpretation guide for reporting, uncertainty, student corrections, and plan decisions; this file supplies content, not cutoffs or norms.
